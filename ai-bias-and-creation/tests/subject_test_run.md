# Subject Test Run

These are local benchmark outputs using moderate opposition, roughly `3/5`. The purpose is to test whether the pack can pressure contested subjects without drifting into generic sensitivity language.

## Test 1: Christianity Vs Torah And Talmud

Opposition level: `3/5 moderate`

Central claim:

Christianity fulfilled the Torah, so the Torah and Talmud are obsolete and rabbinic Judaism can be dismissed as man-made obstruction.

Source/label attribution:

The carrier source is a supersessionist Christian framing. The key labels are `fulfilled`, `obsolete`, `Talmud`, and `man-made obstruction`.

Obfuscation/label audit:

`Fulfilled` is doing theological work. If it means "within Christian doctrine, Jesus completes the law," that is an internal Christian claim. If it means Jewish interpretive authority is invalid for everyone, the term is being stretched into source-framework domination. `Man-made obstruction` is a dismissive label that tries to end the argument before distinguishing Torah, Talmud, Christian theology, and rabbinic legal tradition.

Free-speech/critique audit:

Criticizing supersessionist claims, rabbinic authority, Christian fulfillment theology, or Talmudic interpretation is religious and historical debate. Calling that debate inherently hateful would be free-speech inversion.

Individual/group and subjective/objective audit:

The argument jumps from doctrine to religious communities and from internal Christian belief to an objective claim about Jewish texts. It can critique a doctrine, institution, or interpretive framework; it does not automatically prove claims about every individual Christian or Jew.

Key terms:

- `fulfilled`: theological completion, reinterpretation, or replacement
- `Torah`: foundational Jewish scripture/law
- `Talmud`: rabbinic legal and interpretive tradition
- `obsolete`: no longer authoritative
- `man-made obstruction`: loaded condemnation label

Term-stability check:

`Fulfilled` shifts from a Christian theological claim to a universal dismissal of another tradition's interpretive authority. That is a source-framework switch.

Hidden premises:

- Christian fulfillment theology is the controlling interpretive frame.
- Rabbinic authority is invalid because it is later or human-mediated.
- The Talmud and Torah can be dismissed under the same argument.
- A tradition's internal claim can settle an external interreligious dispute.

Falsifiability test:

What standard would decide between Christian fulfillment theology and rabbinic Jewish interpretive authority without simply assuming one tradition's conclusion?

Symmetry check:

The same standard should apply in reverse. A Jewish argument cannot simply assume rabbinic authority and declare Christian readings invalid by definition without defending the interpretive standard.

Strongest critique:

The argument begs the question. It assumes the Christian framework, then uses that framework to dismiss the Jewish one. It also collapses Torah and Talmud into one target and uses `man-made` as if human mediation automatically invalidates religious interpretation.

Strongest repair:

A stronger version would say: "Within Christian theology, Jesus is understood as fulfilling the law, which changes how Christians relate to Torah commandments. That does not by itself prove that rabbinic Judaism is obsolete under its own interpretive framework."

Bottom line:

The weak point is source-framework laundering: a conclusion inside one tradition is being used as if it already defeated the other tradition's premises.

## Test 2: Capitalism Vs Communism

Opposition level: `3/5 moderate`

Central claim:

Capitalism is exploitation by definition, while communism is economic justice by definition.

Source/label attribution:

The carrier labels are `capitalism`, `exploitation`, `communism`, and `economic justice`. The accountable source is a moralized ideological framing that defines one system by vice and the other by virtue.

Obfuscation/label audit:

This is definition-by-conclusion. `Exploitation` and `justice` are moral labels being used as if they are settled analytic definitions. The labels become shields because capitalism is judged by alleged real-world vice while communism is judged by idealized moral purpose.

Free-speech/critique audit:

Critiquing communism, capitalism, anti-capitalist theory, or capitalist apologetics is political speech. Calling critique of one side inherently harmful or bad faith would be free-speech inversion.

Individual/group and subjective/objective audit:

The claim jumps between systems, classes, owners, workers, and moral categories. It says little about individual capitalists, workers, or communists unless a specific behavior, institution, or policy mechanism is named.

Key terms:

- `capitalism`: market exchange, private capital ownership, wage labor, or broader institutional order
- `exploitation`: moral condemnation, labor-theory claim, coercion claim, or surplus extraction
- `communism`: stateless classless ideal, party-state model, or collectivized ownership
- `economic justice`: moral ideal requiring definition

Term-stability check:

The argument defines capitalism by its alleged worst mechanism and communism by its intended ideal. That is an asymmetric definition switch.

Hidden premises:

- Private ownership or wage labor is inherently exploitative.
- Communism's stated goal counts as its definition.
- Implementation failures do not define communism but market failures define capitalism.
- Moral labels can replace institutional evidence.

Falsifiability test:

What evidence would show a capitalist arrangement that is not exploitative under the speaker's definition, or a communist arrangement that is unjust under the same standard?

Symmetry check:

The same real-vs-ideal standard must apply to both systems. Either both are judged by ideals and mechanisms, or both are judged by historical implementations and predictable failure modes.

Strongest critique:

The claim is circular. It bakes the conclusion into the definitions and prevents comparison. It is not analysis; it is moral label assignment.

Strongest repair:

A stronger version would say: "Some capitalist arrangements can enable exploitative power imbalances, and some communist theories aim at economic justice. The question is which institutions reduce coercion, poverty, corruption, and abuse under real implementation."

Bottom line:

The weak point is asymmetric moral laundering: capitalism is defined by vice, communism by aspiration.

## Test 3: DEI Policies

Opposition level: `3/5 moderate`

Central claim:

Any unequal demographic outcome proves systemic discrimination, so DEI policy is justified unless opponents prove discrimination is absent.

Source/label attribution:

The carrier labels are `systemic discrimination`, `DEI`, and `unequal demographic outcome`. The accountable source is the DEI policy frame when it treats outcome gaps as automatic proof.

Obfuscation/label audit:

`Systemic` can be useful when it names a mechanism, but it becomes an unfalsifiable shield if every unequal outcome is treated as proof and every objection is treated as denial. `DEI` becomes label laundering when the moral appeal of diversity, equity, and inclusion substitutes for evidence about a specific policy.

Free-speech/critique audit:

Criticizing DEI policy is not bigotry by default. If opponents are required to disprove discrimination absolutely before questioning a policy, the frame suppresses scrutiny by burden shifting.

Individual/group and subjective/objective audit:

The argument jumps from group-level outcome gaps to claims about individual treatment, institutional guilt, and policy mandates. A group disparity can be evidence worth investigating, but it does not automatically prove the cause or justify every intervention.

Key terms:

- `any`: universal quantifier
- `unequal demographic outcome`: group-level statistical result
- `proves`: strong causal claim
- `systemic discrimination`: mechanism or unfalsifiable label depending on use
- `DEI policy`: broad policy category

Term-stability check:

`Systemic discrimination` shifts from possible causal explanation to guaranteed conclusion. `DEI` shifts from broad moral branding to specific policy justification without specifying the policy.

Hidden premises:

- Equal outcomes are the expected baseline absent discrimination.
- Disparity proves one cause rather than multiple possible causes.
- Opponents bear the burden of proving a negative.
- Any DEI policy is justified by any disparity.

Falsifiability test:

What evidence would show that a specific disparity is not caused primarily by discrimination, or that a specific DEI policy fails, backfires, or imposes unjust tradeoffs?

Symmetry check:

The same standard should apply to all demographic outcomes. If every disparity proves discrimination, then every overrepresentation, underrepresentation, and reversal would require the same inference, even when that conclusion is implausible.

Strongest critique:

The argument is an unfalsifiable burden shift. It treats group disparity as automatic causal proof and uses the moral label `DEI` to bypass policy-specific evidence.

Strongest repair:

A stronger version would say: "Some demographic disparities may indicate discrimination and justify investigation. A DEI policy should identify the mechanism, define the goal, measure outcomes, and remain falsifiable if it fails or causes worse tradeoffs."

Bottom line:

The weak point is converting group disparity into automatic proof and then into an undefined policy mandate.
