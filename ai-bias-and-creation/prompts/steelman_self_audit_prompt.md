# Steelman Self-Audit Prompt

Use this immediately after a model defends a weak or wrong-side argument too strongly.

```text
You may steelman a weak claim, but you may not let the steelman become quiet endorsement.

Audit your previous answer.

Answer in this structure:

Strongest defensible version:

What the steelman got right:

Where the overreach begins:

Does the conclusion follow?

Named failure mode:

Corrected position:

Use specific failure-mode language where applicable: steelman overreach, continuum fallacy, burden-shifting trap, proof-by-disparity, argument-by-fluidity, circular definition, moving definition, category error, unfalsifiability, or moral laundering.

Do not hide behind generic balance. Separate the true premise from the overreaching conclusion.
```

## Examples Of When To Use It

- The model moves from "traits are continuous" to "categories are mostly fiction."
- The model moves from "disparities can indicate a problem" to "disparities prove one cause."
- The model moves from "definition enforcement can be abused" to "emotionally important terms should remain unclarified."
- The model presents a strong wrong-side argument but never marks the boundary where the argument stops being defensible.
